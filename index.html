Here's the hackathon you'll still be talking about next year. UNSW's No Code Society & MLAI bring you the Sydney edition of the much-loved HealthHack event — bringing clinicians, builders and optimists into the same room for one intense weekend of problem-solving. Prototype real solutions to real healthcare bottlenecks, then pitch them to leaders across health, tech and venture.

Two Tracts, One Mission.

Coding Tract

AI Hospital
Challenge

Build an AI that monitors simulated ward vitals and flags risk early. Perfect for first-timers or clinicians new to machine learning. You'll receive continuous patient vitals data — heart rate, blood pressure, oxygen saturation — from simulated patients. Your task: build a model that monitors in real-time and alerts staff when something looks wrong. Workshops and mentors support all skill levels.

Join Coding Tract
Pitching Tract

EMR Data
Challenge

Work hands-on with de-identified hospital EMR data and industry mentors to tackle high-impact problems from the front lines. Find your problem, break it apart, build your solution, and pitch! You'll work with healthcare mentors through guided interviews to identify critical bottlenecks, then build a compelling demo and pitch to expert judges.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need prior experience in AI or healthcare?
Not at all. The Coding Tract is designed for all skill levels, from newcomers to experienced developers. Mentors and workshops will get you up to speed.
Can I form a team beforehand?
Absolutely. You can pre-form teams, and we encourage it. The Pre-Hack Networking Night on July 17 is the perfect place to find your co-hackers. Solo attendees can also join teams on Day 1.
Is the data real and safe to use?
Yes. All real data is fully de-identified and complies with strict privacy regulations. For the Coding Tract, simulated data ensures a safe learning environment.
What are the team size requirements?
Teams should be a minimum of 2 and a maximum of 6 members. We encourage teams of 5–6 for the full experience.
Will food and coffee be provided?
Yes! Meals and refreshments are provided throughout the hackathon weekend. We've got you covered so you can focus on building.
Can I do both the Coding and Pitching Tracts?
Yes — and we encourage it! Doing both gives you the full HealthHack experience: build a technical solution and pitch your ideas to judges.
Do I need to attend both days?
Day 1 (July 18) is mandatory for all participants. Day 2 (July 19) is also required as that's when pitches and the prize ceremony happen.
What are the prizes?
The winner takes home 10,000 Codex (OpenAI) credits. Additional prizes from our sponsors are TBA — stay tuned for announcements.
Is this only for UNSW students?
Not at all. HealthHack is open to everyone — students from any university, working professionals, clinicians, and community members are all welcome.
Is there an online participation option?
The hackathon is an in-person event. At least one team member must be present on-site. If you have exceptional circumstances, reach out to us.

Who Are We?

No Code Society — UNSW

UNSW's No Code Society is a student-led organisation empowering the next generation of builders, makers and innovators. We run workshops, hackathons and networking events that help anyone — regardless of technical background — learn to build products and solve problems with modern tools. HealthHack Sydney is our flagship event for 2025.

MLAI — Machine Learning & AI

MLAI is a not-for-profit organisation fostering a vibrant community passionate about AI. With over 15,000 members from diverse backgrounds — data scientists, AI developers, healthcare professionals and students — MLAI makes AI accessible, fun and impactful. From monthly lightning talks to large-scale hackathons, MLAI's events are consistently oversubscribed.

UNSW MedSoc

UNSW Medical Society represents the interests of medical students at UNSW Sydney, fostering a supportive community across clinical and academic life. MedSoc is a proud co-organiser of HealthHack Sydney, bridging the gap between medical students, clinicians, and the broader tech and innovation ecosystem.
